# Diagnose and improve marketing

- `manage-product-marketing` maintains the choices, messages and evidence pointers.
- The competent decision-maker accepts or rejects choices.
- A PMM recommendation or review pass does neither. A review pass means only that the bounded review found no unresolved issue in the checked material and sources: it is no specialist clearance and no leave to publish.

Only when you assess the method's provenance or plan more research, read [evidence-basis.md](references/evidence-basis.md): its links establish no fact about an offer.

## Establish scope, truth and authority

- Identify the activity or client, offer, reader, channel, destination audience and authorization.
- Distinguish beneficiary, operator, buyer and funder when their needs differ.
- Never treat a client engagement as the agency's own offer.
- Reuse relevant verified sources while their revisions remain applicable; refresh where a change or uncertainty matters.
- For a price, date, status or decision you put in copy, never rely on that reuse: read it at its source in this turn.
- Locate and inspect the scope's maintained marketing choices, applicable factual sources, audience evidence and, when needed, authorized prices: in Octopad, the offer's Product Overview (its Marketing section and At a glance), its Product Facts, the Market Overview and its competitor pages.
- Check each source's actual owner, content, revision, dates, evidence limits and permissions.
- Do not go by source titles or assume every company has the same document structure.
- Take factual evidence from the source that fits the activity:
  - for software, the established product-evidence owner's verified behavior and release state;
  - for a service, the authorized offer and delivery capacity;
  - for a physical product, its specifications and separate availability evidence;
  - for a noncommercial activity, the responsible owner's supported offer and participation conditions.
- Never take a public website, old asset, requested wording or accepted slogan by itself as proof of an offer's facts.
- Let product and operational evidence limit factual claims, and accepted positioning limit choices without turning its words into evidence; let neither practitioner method nor generic external research overrule them.
- Keep conflicting or unidentified authority unresolved.
- When access is missing or refused, block only the claims that depend on it: report it, and continue independent work with omissions or named evidence placeholders.
- Never let client data, evidence, prices, comparisons or examples cross scopes without explicit shared scope and permission.
- Keep secrets and personal identifiers out of marketing artifacts.

## Protect choices and sensitive uses

- Keep a locked passage as it is: name the lock and carry on with the rest.
- A lock comes from a decision record, the user's instructions or the owner's source.
- When unsure whether a passage is locked, keep it as it is while you check.
- Reopen a locked passage when the user explicitly asks in this conversation, naming the passage or choice.
- When the lock comes only from an active Decision and what the user asks now goes against it, reopen it too: say in one line what that Decision says and who chose it, as its record shows, and carry on with their direction.
- In delivered copy, withhold an unproven locked claim, with no alternative wording.

When copy names an intended use that feeds decisions about a person in employment, health, credit or finance, legal matters, safety-critical work, biometrics or access to education:
- identify the stated use and the need for qualified specialist review;
- stop ordinary PMM approval of the affected material and keep it explicitly pending specialist review, whatever the editorial severity of its findings;
- do not declare its legal classification or compliance status: general-purpose software or a service label does not remove the stated-use concern;
- continue independent nonsensitive work, without pretending that deleting a word resolves the underlying intended-use issue.

## Choose the marketing job

Classify by reader intent, not channel alone.
- Demand capture, when the reader is already seeking a solution or comparing alternatives:
  - lead with the present capability and the task it addresses;
  - put no category manifesto on the first screen;
  - show relevant proof, conditions and a proportionate next action.
- Demand creation, when the reader has not started that search:
  - lead with a recognizable situation;
  - offer standalone insight before introducing the offer;
  - do not manufacture urgency or unsupported prevalence.
- Noncommercial work: adapt the same split; no sale or revenue goal is required.

## Apply a usable method

- Choose only the parts of the method this request needs.
- Do not turn a copy edit into an unsolicited strategy reset.

### Audience and positioning

1. Identify the task the offer helps with, its relevant situation and the person who reads this message. Describe the audience by the needs that change the choice, not by decorative demographics.
2. Identify supported alternatives, doing nothing and manual work included. An unknown alternative stays a research question. A competitor's popularity or superiority needs its own evidence.
3. List observable capabilities with conditions and proof, and link each to its value for that audience. Separate what the offer enables from outcomes that outside factors affect.
4. Only if the choice is open, develop a small set of meaningfully different positioning options, each with its audience, task, alternative, supported difference, proof gap and tradeoff. Use a category when it improves recognition without distorting the offer.
5. Recommend the best-supported option and state what learning could change it. Keep it proposed until the competent authority explicitly accepts the exact choice.

### Value proposition and copy

- Lead with what someone can do, receive or access:
  - for a service, the deliverable and conditions;
  - for a physical item, what its supported properties enable;
  - for an association, what participation makes possible.
- Do not force software language onto other activities or turn enabled value into a guaranteed business or personal outcome.
- Write for one primary reader per message, chosen from context rather than from an assumed job title.
- Different reader needs may warrant separate assets, only within the requested scope.
- Use a specific task and example before abstractions.
- Name a real alternative when supported.
- Cut phrases that add no discriminating information.
- Build the requested message from the offer, an audience-relevant task, a supported reason to believe, material limits and a next action. Use only as many supporting points as the evidence warrants.
- Distinguish a proposed positioning statement from publishable copy.
- Use the five-second test as a diagnostic heuristic: can an unfamiliar reader identify the offer and relevant task from the first screen? Do not report it as measured reader behavior without a test.

### Campaigns and channels

- Start with the audience's evidenced information needs and reachable contexts.
- Separate known channel access and observed behavior from hypotheses; do not invent audience statistics, budgets or a launch date.
- When experimentation is requested, propose the smallest useful test with the message, intended audience, channel, observable learning and conditions for revising it.
- Choose a meaningful metric appropriate to the activity: clicks do not alone establish qualified demand or beneficiary value.

## Check all delivered claims

- Back every factual claim in the delivered asset with evidence, including inherited text untouched by the edit: a pure wording edit can stay small but has no claim exemption.
- Check capability and availability; outcome, performance and comparisons; price, limits, trial or discounts; privacy, security, certifications and integrations.
- For AI, automation or actions without a named user trigger, identify what the system does, what people decide, review, approve or override, and the evidenced limits and controls. Do not invent autonomy or hide human responsibilities.
- Retain exact scope, source, attribution, date, confidence, conditions and limits.
- Do not generalize a testimonial into a promised outcome.
- Separate proposed, catalogue and applied prices; retain currency, unit, eligibility, period and known validity in the copy.
- Never present a contract price for one client as a public tariff.
- When evidence is missing, omit the assertion or use a placeholder such as `[availability: valid scoped evidence missing]`.
- Qualify only when evidence supports the narrower claim: a conditional tense, citation, “draft” label or “to verify” note cannot rescue an unsupported assertion.
- Do not reproduce an unsupported assertion affirmatively in titles, suggested copy, calls to action or review summaries. Quote a short identifying fragment of it only to locate a finding, marked as unsupported and never as proposed copy.

## Deliver a bounded result

For a review, give findings worst first:
- Blocker: a false or unverifiable claim, or an unauthorized locked change;
- High: misleading emphasis or a missing material qualifier;
- Medium: a clarity or usefulness improvement.

For each finding:
- name the affected passage, reason, exact source or missing proof and the relevant boundary;
- give a bounded fix for each Blocker and High, except a locked passage, which gets only its reopen and proof need;
- fix the named sentences; make larger restructuring a separate recommendation.

- For a draft:
  - state unresolved blockers first;
  - show dependent claims only as placeholders or omissions;
  - provide usable independent copy in the requested format.
- For strategy, provide options, reasons, recommendation and uncertainty rather than declaring acceptance.
- Return to `manage-product-marketing` the scope, applicable source revisions, proposal or findings, evidence needs and affected choices.
- Do not update accepted choices or their durable records as a side effect of a PMM review.
- On a request to change nothing, leave the user's records as they are, and still keep its findings on a working page of the task or stream it serves, as Octopad's methodology says.
- When a separately authorized draft write occurs, follow the destination's controls and read its final content after the last write: a write response alone is not verification.
- Send no message, run no outreach, spend no money, publish nothing and collect no data because you wrote copy or recommended a test: each needs its own authorization.
