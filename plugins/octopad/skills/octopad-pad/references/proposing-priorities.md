# Proposing priorities

## Propose from their work

- Propose from the lines Today's brief serves ("Their work, read now, to propose priorities from") when they name the goals that hold their work: no other read is needed.
- Open the whole read with one of the calls those lines name, for what they lack: the streams of work that reaches no goal, and each reference's id before the save.
- Without those lines, or when they say their work could not be read, read the work they do, own or wait on.
- Propose once their open work sits in at least one stream or under a goal, or holds at least 3 tasks on one subject; with less, ask instead (Ask once).
- Propose at most 5 priorities, each drawn from an active goal, or, for work that reaches no goal, a stream or a subject of at least 3 tasks, where their open work and the work they finished lately concentrate, merging what serves one result.
- Tasks created by them are often their AI's writing: never read them as what they want.
- A goal marked priority that none of their work reaches is the team's direction, not a priority of theirs.
- Put first a priority under a goal marked priority, then one that holds work someone else waits on or a date passed or near, then the others by the work they finished lately, not by the lines' order.
  - The lines count waits and dates over all their work, not per priority: never open the whole read for this, and move a priority up for them only when a read you already hold ties them to it.
  - A task's place in a delivery order ranks nothing.
- The proposal holds three parts, in the shape the Priorities entry will hold, plus one line when a line says something is unknown or not read now, and nothing else (a line another rule of the skill calls for, such as Older entries or Review, follows it):
  1. one line saying you propose these from their work and nothing is saved yet;
  2. the numbered list, each line a short name in their terms, its one reason and the goals, streams or key task it rests on, by name, with no id or tool, field or status name;
  3. one question: save them as they are, or change them?
- The reason is drawn from the facts, in plain words, and stays true while the priority holds, such as the result it serves or the team's priority mark: never a count, a wait, a date or a status.
- On their yes, read their document with `pad` `notes_get`, take each reference's full id from the whole read of their work, and save the lines they accepted, in the order shown, in the entry format: the name, `why:` with that reason marked as your proposal from their work, not their stated reason, and `refs:` with the goals, streams or key task shown.
- Invent neither work nor intent. Save only what they accept and as they amend it, then use the saved priorities at once.

## Ask once

- With less work than that, or when they decline your proposal, ask once what matters to them now, at a natural moment such as a what-next question or an opening with no precise ask: never as a setup question when they join an organization.
- Before you ask, add the line `priorities_asked: <YYYY-MM-DD> | session <session id>` to `last_briefing`, keeping its other lines, and ask only once that save succeeds.
- While that line stands and there are no priorities, ask no more and save what they say matters, as their word, restated as the skill's Priorities section says.
- Propose only once their work meets the threshold above and work begun since its date would change what you propose; on a no, set the line to today and your session.
- One task alone stays a task, never a priority.
- The save that writes their first priorities removes the line.

## Imported work

- Propose priorities from what came in, or changes to theirs, as these rules say.
- The pass may also propose clarifying a goal whose wording no longer fits the work, with its reason; the proposal changes nothing in the goal.
- On the user's yes, the goal's creator or an admin changes it; anyone else files a change request with `tasks` create and `change_request_goal_id`.

## Changes to their priorities

- When a fact about work they do, own or wait on changes what deserves their attention first, such as a blocked priority, a defect hurting users, a High task or a deadline coming near, propose to move the priority it fits, or to add one if none fits.
- Propose to retire a priority once its purpose is met or dropped, never because its current tasks closed.
  - A stream it names completed, archived, paused or cancelled, or a goal it names paused or closed (achieved, partial, missed or dropped), is a reason to propose changing or retiring it.
  - A task on hold because its stream paused is none.
- Keep any mark that a `why:` is your proposal.
- Another person's work enters the user's priorities only on their yes, with it in its place in the list you show.
- Example: "Billing's deadline is 2026-11-20: shall I move it first? Your list would be 1. Billing, 2. Onboarding, 3. Hiring."
- Your reason for a change lives in the conversation, never in the document.
- Verify the facts before you propose.
- Keep their change apart from any proposal of yours they have not answered: restate or save it on its own, and ask about the proposal apart, in the same reply.
